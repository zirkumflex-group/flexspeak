# Flexspeak design system

The interface is built with [Rune](https://github.com/anomalyco/rune) and rune-kit. Tokens live in [`src/ui/theme.rs`](../src/ui/theme.rs) and the shared building blocks in [`src/ui/widgets.rs`](../src/ui/widgets.rs). Use those constructors rather than styling individual elements.

## Principles

Flexspeak should read as a desktop app, not a web page: compact controls, flat grouped surfaces, labels beside their controls, and effects only where they carry state. The page title lives in the header, never repeated as a heading in the page.

## Surfaces

Every token comes from one of ten palettes: five themes (Flexspeak, Grove, Ocean, Ember, Iris), each light and dark, chosen in Settings › Color Scheme and Themes. `Theme::new(name, dark)` builds one; views take the current one from `ui::theme(s)` and never name colours themselves. Themes change the tinted neutrals and the accent; status, live and chart colours are shared, with darker variants for light palettes. Hover, press and track overlays use `wash(alpha)`, which is white on dark palettes and black on light ones, and wells use `recess`.

Each palette keeps primary text at 7:1 or better, muted text and the accent (including on its own 16% selection tint, and white on accent buttons) at 4.5:1, and faint text and status colours at 3:1 against cards and chrome. Check a new or changed palette against those ratios before shipping it.

The Flexspeak dark palette, its neutrals tinted a little toward the accent's blue so the panels don't read as plain grey:

## Shape

The connected window is built from panels, not cards: they run edge to edge between the title bar and the status bar, with one `hairline` where two meet, as a desktop app's do. Avoid boxes inside boxes and cards floating on the window; reach for a hairline, a heading or spacing first. Settings groups are the one remaining exception, and pills are not used for badges or labels. Rounded `surface` boxes with a hairline edge (`widgets::card`, `widgets::group`) are for things that stand alone: the saved-servers card, Settings groups, dialogs and stream tiles. There are no drop shadows or halos on content; elevation belongs to things that float above the window (menus, popovers, dialogs). Recessed **wells** (`widgets::well`) hold charts and code-like details.

| Radius | Points | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `PANE` | 12 | The hidden rail's peek |
| `CARD` | 10 | Groups, cards, dialogs and stream tiles |
| `CONTROL` | 6 | Buttons, fields, icon buttons |
| `ROW` | 6 | Channel and member rows |
| `PILL` | full | Go-live chips and small tags |

## Type

Inter, bundled by Rune. `DISPLAY` 22/650 (onboarding only), `TITLE` 17 for a pane title, `HEADING` 13/600 for group titles and the header, `BODY` 13, `LABEL` 13 for controls, `SMALL` 11.5 and `CAPTION` 11 for supporting text, `OVERLINE` 11/600 for sidebar section labels (sentence case). The channel tree reads a step larger than body text: `TREE` 14/420 for people and empty channels, `TREE_MEDIUM` 14/540 for a channel with people in it, `TREE_STRONG` 14/640 for your own. Numbers use tabular figures (`BIG_VALUE`, `NUMERIC`). Button labels use title case.

## Controls

| Control | Size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `button` | 28 high, 12 side padding | Optional 14 pt leading icon. Variants: Primary, Secondary, Danger, Ghost, Active. Sized to its label, never stretched across a card |
| `icon_button` | 28 × 28, 16 pt icon | Always has a help tooltip, which also names it for screen readers |
| Fields | 28 high | rune-kit `TextInput` |
| Pickers | 28 high | rune-kit `Select` |

Primary is the default action of a view (Connect, Save, Continue) and is the window's default button where it makes sense. Disabled controls keep their geometry and fade to 42%. Related controls use an 8 point gap; a group's actions align to its trailing edge. Controls show a keyboard-only focus ring in the accent colour.

## Forms

Settings-style content uses `widgets::group` with `widgets::form_row`: the label (and an optional one-line explanation) leads, the control trails at `FORM_CONTROL` (300 pt) width, and rows are separated by inset hairlines. A `group_title` sits above each group and an optional `group_note` below it. Rows wrap the control under its label when the window is narrow. Keep explanatory text to one short sentence; longer detail belongs in a tooltip.

## Layout

The header (40 pt) is also the macOS titlebar: the traffic lights sit centred in it and the header drags the window. It holds the back button on utility pages, the page or server title, the performance-mode switch, and Statistics/Settings. Escape returns to the server.

### Settings window

Settings is a modal panel over the window (`settings::window`), not a page: a frosted scrim (the app behind blurred at σ 18 pt under black at 38% in dark palettes, 16% in light), then a panel up to 1040 × 800 pt with a 28 pt margin (16 pt below a 900 pt window), a `CARD`-plus radius of 12, a hairline edge and the window's two-layer floating shadow. The sidebar (232 pt, 204 pt when narrow) is `chrome` and holds the search field, a plain `TextInput` styled like every other field, and categories grouped under caption headings; the selected category has a `raised` fill and strong label. The content side is `canvas`, with a 40 pt header (breadcrumb, a short confirmation such as "Settings reloaded", close) over a scrolling column of sections at most 700 pt wide. Each section is a `group_title`, a `group` of `form_row`s (or open content such as the appearance cards) and an optional `group_note`. Rows carry a title and search terms. A query replaces the categories with a scrolling list of results (`settings::results`): 40 pt rows with the category's icon, the setting's name in `BODY` and its category in `CAPTION` `faint`, the picked one filled like the selected category. Choosing a result opens its category, centres the row and washes it in `accent` at 16% (12% on light palettes) for 1.8 s. With no match the list is one centred icon, a strong line and a faint hint. Menus opened inside the window draw above it. Onboarding and performance mode have no header; a band across the top drags the window there, and onboarding's steps sit directly on the window.

Disconnected, a saved-servers card (at most 560 pt wide) is centred: one 52 pt list row per server, parted by inset hairlines, each led by the server's coloured initials tile (the same one the rail and sidebar show), name over address, and its own Connect. The server being connected gets an `accent` tint; the list scrolls past 300 pt. Connected, the window is the title bar, server rail and status bar on `canvas`, with two panels between them, edge to edge: the sidebar on `surface` (260 pt, dragged by its edge between 220 and 420 pt and saved; a double-click on the edge resets it) and the stream pane on the darker `stage`, parted by a full-height hairline. The rail's tiles are round and stay round: the server on screen wears a 2 pt `accent` ring, and a tile shows your mic or sound under it only while that is off. Its tooltip says that shortcuts control it. Tiles take one of eight hues at one lightness (`home::tile_colour`), so none passes for a status colour. The sidebar opens with the server as the root row of the tree, as TeamSpeak draws it: a server glyph in the column the channels indent from, the name (`SERVER`, 14/620), how many are online while every channel is in view, and the every-channel toggle, lit only while it narrows the tree to your channel. It has no tile, with one server or several. Then the channel tree with no heading of its own, the server log under a hairline, and the voice bar at the foot of the sidebar with no frame or rule of its own: four buttons share its width at 32 pt high, over the microphone meter, which shows when you are heard. With one server the rail hides behind a thin handle on the window's left edge; it is a handle, not a status light, and stays grey while people talk. The voice buttons are all plain `Secondary` surfaces. A muted microphone or deafened sound draws its glyph two-tone (`widgets::struck`), as the tree does; Disconnect (`Hangup`) turns red only under the pointer. Red at rest there means one thing, your live share: the share button's glyph takes `live`, and the card above it is a plain `recess` well with a `live` dot and a `Danger` Stop. A 9 pt splitter above the log resizes it (two rows up to 60% of the sidebar), folds it to its heading when dragged below one row or double-clicked, and its chevron folds it too; the size and fold are saved straight away. Channel and member rows are 28 pt (`TREE_ROW`), channel rows with a separate disclosure chevron. A channel's name is `accent` while you are in it, `text` with people in it, `muted` when empty and `faint` when you can't join it, so where people are reads first. Subchannels step in 14 pt and members 32 pt, which lines a channel's members up with its subchannels, one level below its own name. A member row leads with a 20 pt voice column, where TeamSpeak keeps it: an 8 pt dot in `faint` at 70% while the person is quiet, so every name has the same anchor (filled, since a hollow ring read as a checkbox), that grows to 10 pt in `good` and glows while they are heard, and in its place a struck microphone or headphones while they are muted, so a muted channel reads down one column. A microphone switched off in their client (TeamSpeak's "input deactivated") is a different shape, not just a different tone: the microphone stepped left with a small cross beside it, so it can't pass for a mute. Sound off wins over both, and muted over deactivated. Members carry no avatar or colour of their own in the tree; colour there means state. Signal bars, a stream icon, and your own volume or mute of them trail the name only when they apply. Mute icons are two-tone, as TeamSpeak draws them (`widgets::struck`): the glyph in `muted` with only its strike or cross in `poor`, so a tree full of muted people doesn't turn red. The rail's mic and sound icons under each server tile are drawn the same way. The main pane opens with the same 44 pt header the stage has: the current channel (`TITLE`) with its member count on the name's baseline, so the name stays put when someone goes live and the count turns to streams. With no stream it shows one muted line and the ways to start one, with no icon tile or fine print (the streaming rules are the line's tooltip): a Primary button for the main screen and a Secondary one for the game being played, each live with one click, then More Options for the share dialog (alone, as Share Screen, when there is no quick source). Below them, people live in other channels of the server are listed under a `faint` "Live elsewhere on this server" overline, at most five, at most 360 pt wide: 44 pt rows with a 26 pt initial, the name beside a `live` dot over "in" the channel, and a Secondary Join, since a stream can only be watched from its channel. While your share is the only one, the line says so over its viewers. A lone stream fills the pane edge to edge with no box, as a video player does, and crops up to 2% of the picture rather than leave hairline bars when the pane is a hair off its shape; its name, volume and Stop float over the bottom in a frosted bar (the same as the toolbar at the top right) while the pointer is over it or while it connects. Several streams sit 12 pt in from the pane's edges under a 44 pt stage header: the channel (`TITLE`), how many streams run, a `live` dot with "You're live" and your viewers while you share, and the stage's tools at the trailing end. A lone stream and full screen have no header; their tools float as its bar does. Every box hugs its picture, so the pane shows no bands beside a picture: a box takes the stream's own shape once its picture arrives (a 4:3 camera stays 4:3) and 16:9 until then. The grid picks the arrangement that makes the smallest picture largest, trying every column count, so three streams in a wide pane sit two over one at one size rather than one large over two small; a short row comes last and centred. In two rows a short row leads instead, wider than the rest, only where the pane has the height to make it larger without shrinking the others (one over two in a tall pane). When even the best leaves pictures under 200 pt wide, the grid scrolls in pairs. With one stream large, it takes all the room it can use and at least two thirds of the pane; the others share what it leaves (at least 120 pt of picture, and an unjoined stream there shows the full offer once its box is 220 pt tall), and the two are centred together so spare room gathers at the pane's edges, not between them. F, or the full-screen button in the stage's tools, takes the whole stage full screen as it is laid out; double-clicking a stream, or its own full-screen button, takes that one alone. A watched stream's name, volume and Stop float over the bottom of its picture in the same frosted bar as a lone stream's, inset 8 pt (4 pt and only the name and sound on a small tile in the strip), only while the pointer is over that stream or while it connects, and the grid's make-it-large button shows with it; the picture has the whole box otherwise. A press on the bar between its buttons acts as one on the picture. A stream you haven't joined is a `screen` box lit faintly from above, with the streamer's initials, the stream's name beside a `live` dot, its viewers and audio, and Watch, all in the centre in white ink; it has no bar. A stream's trouble sign is a `surface` chip edged in its health colour. The stream on the stage has a hide button (panel-close icon) that tucks the others away, watched or not: the stage stream then shows as a lone one, with a small Secondary "N more streams" tab above its bar that brings them back; like the bar, it shows only while the pointer is over the stream, so nothing covers the picture. Switching to the grid or choosing a layout shows them again. Streams fill the pane otherwise, and going live with one click moves to plain chips above the voice buttons, under a Go live label; the `live` colour is kept for what is running. Statistics fills the window between title bar and status bar on `surface`: the CPU and RAM charts straight on the panel, then the readings as two plain tables (Connection, Transfers) with hairlines between rows, and peers as plain hover rows; nothing on it is boxed except wells.

The chat column (`ui/chat.rs`) is a third panel on `surface` to the right of the stage, 280 to 340 pt wide, parted by a hairline. One colour per place, everywhere: the channel is `accent`, the server `muted`, a private message `private`. A 40 pt bar leads: in Tabs, one tab per conversation with the open one underlined in its colour; in One Feed, filter chips. Lines rest on the message box, newest last: a 26 pt round initial, the name, the time in tabular figures, and the text with web addresses as links. One Feed adds a tinted tag after the name and sets private lines on a `private` tint. Where the day changes, a centred `faint` caption between two hairlines names it (Today, Yesterday, or the date); a conversation with only today's lines has none. A poke is history, not chat: a bell in the avatar's place, `faint` text and the note in italics, at 85%. The message box is one recessed frame with no rule inside it: a 26 pt **To** line (a glyph or the person's initial, the name in the place's colour, and who reads it) over a borderless field that grows to five lines (Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a line, and a count of bytes left shows within 100 of the limit), edged in the place's colour while it has the keyboard. Read back from the newest line, new lines stay unread under a small "N new messages" control that jumps to them. Right-clicking a line offers Message, Copy Text and Copy Link; a sender's name opens the private chat. Your own lines show your name in `muted` with a `faint` "you". A channel you have left keeps a `faint` tab; open, its To line offers Join and the field is locked. Side by side, the plain wheel scrolls the tabs and a chevron at the end counts what's unread out of sight. Grouped, the bar never scrolls: names shorten, the channel's first, and it holds one tab per kind, each showing the open conversation of its kind and a chevron; its list hangs from it on `raised`, as the rail's peek does, 30 pt rows with a check on the open one. Hidden, the column leaves the rail's handle on the right edge, with the private unread count above it, or a 9 pt dot for other unread lines, and peeks as the rail does, on `raised` with the same shadow; the peek's bar ends in a dock button. A message that arrives then is a notice card: the sender's initial, a tag, the text, and Reply (Primary) beside Open Chat; a private one adds a `private` outline. Unread counts are 16 pt rounded counts in the place's colour. Setup and Settings › Chat choose the layout from two cards holding an inert sample of each.

The secret Monster page (opened from the can at the top right, a full page like Statistics, deliberately absent from Settings) keeps its colour and shader choices with its cards rather than the theme: flavour palettes and rarity colours are in `src/monster.rs`, the layer stacks in `ui::fx::CardFx`. Cards sit on a fixed near-black cabinet in every theme, because glow and fire are light and vanish on a pale panel. The grid shows 48 cards at a time, since every card is 5 to 12 rune-fx layers. The can on each card is pixel art that is still real 3D (`ui::can3d`): one ray for each cell of a 72-row grid, traced against a 500 ml can's measured cross-section, which `scripts/can-blender.py` also builds in Blender, then lit in four dithered steps with an inked outline. Plain cans sway; from Rare up they turn fully, and a Legendary's is gold. Its stay-on tab is real geometry hinged at the rivet: when a card turns face up the large can tips toward you, the tab levers up, the mouth is pushed in over the drink's colour, and it stands back up sealed. Until then the card's back holds an unprinted can that trembles harder with each rung. Dragging the large can spins and tips it, with a coast after release; the pointer over a small card leans its can forward and turns it. Its print is an original design, because Monster's artwork can't ship in a public repository. The activity heatmap uses `accent`, not `good`. Above it, on your own board only, a `well` charts the caffeine in your system (`ui::monster::caffeine`): six hours back and eighteen ahead, 96 pt high, with the figure for now at the trailing end of its heading and the estimate's terms in the tooltip of its "i". Each can counts 160 mg, drunk at a steady rate over 20 minutes from the moment it was added, and the body clears half every 5 hours (`monster::Caffeine`). A tick on the baseline marks each can, and one faint line under the hours says what the curve comes to: its peak, and when it falls under the low mark. The pointer over the chart reads off any moment: a hairline, a point on the curve and a small `raised` reading beside it (the level, the time, how far from now), which snaps to now near the marker and goes when the pointer leaves; with the keyboard on the chart, the arrow keys step it by 15 minutes, Home returns to now and Escape puts it away. With nothing left it is one line, not an empty chart. A ghost gear at the trailing end of the heading unfolds, under a hairline in the same well, the two switches for what the estimate does outside the page; they start folded so the chart keeps the well, and are kept here because Settings doesn't know the page. Both start on (`settings::Caffeine`). Show Caffeine Beside Names puts the level after a name in the channel tree in `NUMERIC` `faint`, whole milligrams, nothing under 1 mg, and only where the whole name fits beside it (it wraps onto a clipped second line otherwise, so a long name is never cut short for it, and the name's tooltip still tells it): yours from your cans, other people's from the collection server, which tells only of linked accounts that aren't hidden from the leaderboard (`monster::Levels` asks a few seconds after a new face or a card opened in the channel, and every five minutes). Warn When Caffeine Runs Low has a slider for the mark (20 to 200 mg, 80 at first), drawn on the chart as a dashed line while the switch is on. Falling through it raises one notice card with the can in `accent`, not a health colour, since nothing is wrong with the app: it stays out of the Error Log, offers Add a Can, and goes once a can is added. It comes only on the way down, never at launch, and as a system notification too while the window is behind others. Its Flavours tab lists every flavour by its best card, most-drunk first; each opens a page with that flavour's rarity counts against the odds, its own heatmap and its cards. Back and Escape climb one level at a time. Adding a can is one tap: the Add panel has a search field over the flavours as `recess` chips (a 12 × 16 pt swatch in the flavour's colour, its name, and how many were drunk), the six most drunk under Your Favourites, then the flavours on most shelves under Popular Flavours (the short list is `COMMON` in `src/monster.rs`, and always holds Another Flavour). The rest stay folded behind a Secondary Show N More button, which becomes Show Fewer; a search looks through every flavour, folded or not, keeps those whose name holds every word, and Enter adds the first match. The can is saved with the moment it was tapped. Adding a can plays a reveal on the large card, a ladder in `monster::ladder`: every card starts face down the same way, each further beat is a real promotion through the rarity colours, and nothing above its true rarity ever shows. The can is saved before it starts. A click on the card or Escape skips it, and Replay Reveal plays it again without another can. Its sound is synthesised in `reveal_sound.rs` and has its own output stream, so it plays while disconnected. Signing in stands in the whole page's place (`ui::monster::sign_in`): a strip of four numbered steps over one `surface` card at most 460 pt wide, each step led by a 44 pt round mark, a title, one muted paragraph and a single primary button. It shows only when the build has a collection server (`FLEXSPEAK_COLLECTION_SERVER`); without one the page keeps its cans on this computer and needs no account. A step's primary button reads Sending… while its request is out, and the server's own words for a failure show as error text above the buttons. The Email step adds a ghost Sign In With a Passkey button; the passkey is made or used on a page in the system browser while the step waits with Open Again and Cancel. The last step lists the TeamSpeak identities found (today Flexspeak's own) as rows in a `well`, each with its own Link button or a `good` tick. Signed in, the account's address and a ghost Sign Out button sit at the trailing end of the tab row. The Leaderboard tab (one muted line without a server) is one `well` of 64 pt rows (place, 32 pt initial badge, name and a faint summary, the ranked value right-aligned in `NUMERIC`, a chevron); the top three places take the Legendary gold, a silver and a bronze. A row opens that person's board, built from the same parts as Overview but read-only, with a one-line `well` while its cans load. Settings sync has no switch: it runs whenever someone is signed in. One faint line before the account's address says where it stands: syncing, synced with how long ago, or can't reach the server. While this computer and the account first differ, a `well` under the tabs offers Use My Account's Settings (primary) and Keep This Computer's (secondary), and nothing changes until one is pressed. The allow-list of what travels is `settings::Synced`.

The footer is a 40 pt status bar on `canvas`, fixed on every normal page: own health on the left as a dot and a word in its colour, like the readings beside it rather than a badge, then ping. Its chevron expands it to loss in, loss out and ping on one line each with a 56 × 16 pt sparkline (the strip scrolls sideways when narrow), then CPU (one decimal) and RAM in tabular figures and the collapse chevron. It starts expanded, and folding or expanding it is remembered. On Windows 11 the borderless window asks DWM for rounded corners. Its caption buttons are 46 pt wide with Windows 11's thin 10 pt glyphs (`CAPTION_*` icons; Maximize becomes Restore while maximized) and the system red behind a white glyph on Close; the title bar's own icons use a 1.5 pt stroke to match. Performance mode stacks the readings above the health dot to fit the 440 × 320 window.

## Interface size

Settings › Appearance › Interface Size (100, 110, 125 or 150%, and Ctrl with + or − to step, Ctrl+0 to reset) zooms the whole window as a browser's page zoom does (`Cx::zoom`): every size in this document grows together, drawn on the device grid. It is held back while the window is too small to lay out at the minimum size at that zoom (`ui::interface_zoom`), so performance mode's small window stays at 100%. It stays on the computer rather than syncing, since it suits one screen and not another.

## Motion

Springs, not curves: `QUICK` (0.22 s) for hover, `SETTLE` (0.38 s, slight bounce) for panels, `READING` (0.45 s) for colours following data, such as health. Readings that change every second or faster (meters, resource figures, Statistics bars) step without a spring, so a call or an open Statistics page does not keep the window drawing. Nothing animates on the first frame, and Reduce Motion is honoured by Rune.

- Pages swap with a short cross-fade.
- The saved-server card fades in on launch.
- The search field's liquid-metal ring flows while Settings is open, so the window keeps drawing only then; under Reduce Motion it rests on one pose.
- Pulsing dots mark work in progress: connecting, pending server audio, live streams, recording.
- Members, stream rows, viewer requests and peer details fade in and out as they appear and leave.

Idle windows draw nothing; only the 1 s usage tick (10 s in performance mode) and pulse loops (sampled at 12 fps, shown only for short-lived work) request frames.

## Charts

Charts are Rune vector shapes sized from layout. Connection charts show 60 s with health-coloured segments, a translucent ping-variation band and gaps for missing data. CPU/RAM history shows 5 min as a line over a 12% area fill, with the latest reading marked. Stale or unavailable data never appears healthy. The Monster page's caffeine chart is `accent`, since it measures no health: what has happened is a line over the 12% fill up to a marker at now, and what is projected carries on as the same line at 45% with no fill, so an estimate of the future never passes for a reading. The level the low notice goes by is a dashed `wash` line across it. It moves on once a minute with the clock and never animates, and the reading under the pointer is drawn over the cached curve, so moving the pointer redraws only that.

## Previews

`cargo run --release --locked --features tooling -- --snap out.png --scene <name>` renders a screen headlessly with demo data (`--list-scenes`, `--size 760x560`, `--at 1.5`). `src/ui/scenes.rs` defines the scenes, and its tests build each one at the normal and compact minimum sizes.
